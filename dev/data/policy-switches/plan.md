# Matched-switch policy intervention

At tick 600 in each of the same 100 seed blocks, select disjoint pairs of individuals with different original policies. The same individuals change policy in both arms. The sham swaps the two policies in each pair and therefore preserves all policy counts. The treatment assigns policies to those individuals to reduce the sum of squared policy counts, accepting a pair only when this makes the counts more even. Both arms reset policy caches identically and preserve physical state and the simulation RNG. Record the number of switches and policy counts for every seed, including zero-switch cases.

The primary outcome is the paired difference in population size at tick 1,000. Track population size, occupancy density on the fixed grid, age bins 0–49, 50–99, 100–149 and 150+, mean age, policy frequencies and grass every 50 ticks. Age and density are exactly matched at the intervention but are allowed to change afterwards as downstream outcomes. Compare both arms with the same seed-matched mean of eight single-policy controls. Report paired 95% intervals and bootstrap sensitivity; intermediate time points are exploratory. Check matching, policy counts, trajectory replay, endpoint viability and all seed denominators. The treatment is an artificial intervention and might not achieve a fully even mix, so document actual changes and persistent diversity.
