# Genetic and behavioural diversity: research summary

Bhavya's 500-run sampled-environment study raised a useful question: does greater genetic variation produce more varied behaviour and better population performance? The follow-up work supports a more nuanced answer.

1. In the 500-run analysis, 309 simulations survived and 191 went extinct. Among the 305 surviving runs with at least two agents, mutation and final population size were associated with the genetic diversity measure. These are exploratory associations, not a causal separation of environmental and demographic effects.
2. The original 500-run analysis and the matched-founder and first behavioural-diversity experiments used a legacy kernel that incorrectly limited an individual to one focal reproductive episode. Their numerical results need rerunning before being interpreted as results of the corrected model. The issue and reproduction correction are documented in `dev/data/diversity-vald/analysis.md` and `dev/dev-log/after-task/2026-11-14-iteroparous-reset.md`.
3. In the corrected-model performance experiments, greater mutation produced clear differences in action preferences, but the average food and birth-rate gains were small, with lower offspring survival and smaller final populations. The mutation comparison does not isolate diversity itself.
4. Equal-density policy mixtures showed no material short-term foraging advantage over equally weighted single-policy controls in the tested worlds. Some population advantages appeared at particular times and conditions, but they did not generalize consistently to the longer, lower-bottleneck settings.
5. The food-by-threshold interaction in mixture population advantage was uncertain at both 300- and 400-tick maximum lifespans, including a 100-source matched comparison and a separate 100-source replication at 400 ticks. Birth-age components shifted toward the broad older-parent band, but this does not demonstrate a causal mechanism. At lifespan 400, the older band includes ages 300–399, which could not occur at lifespan 300.

The practical conclusion is that greater behavioural diversity did not consistently improve foraging or population performance in these specific simulations. Viability and lifespan, food, ploidy, density, founder ages and the particular behavioural policies all matter for interpretation. We should not claim a general lack of benefit, the proposed diversity curve, or a natural-population effect. The original 500-run and matched-founder analyses still need corrected-kernel replication before being used as evidence about the current model.
