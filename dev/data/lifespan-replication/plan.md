# Independent lifespan replication

## Question and design

Repeat the previous food-by-reproductive-threshold and age-specific birth comparison with assay maximum age 400 rather than 300 ticks. The 400 setting passed the earlier five-source candidate screen, where all 360 assays avoided sub-20 dips, ended viable and avoided the 700-agent cap. Before any main results, validate that same setting on five disjoint sources 86001–86005. The source training world remains at maximum age 200, threshold 80 and 100 founders. Assays use 96 founders, thresholds 80 and 120, original and limited food, fixed and staggered founder ages, and one mixture plus eight equally weighted single-policy controls. Mutation is disabled during assays. The changed lifespan applies identically to all assay treatments and directly affects survival, birth timing and crowding; it is not a pure intervention on population stability.

Prespecify the prior study's gates: every source ends viable and remains at or above 20; within each threshold, at least 95% of assays end viable and 95% remain at or above 20, with under 1% of observations at the hard cap. Check all treatment subgroups separately, source panel distinctness and behaviour, source and initial-state matching, one-agent-per-cell assertions, population conservation and age-birth ledgers. Independently reconstruct pilot cohorts and time-to-birth from individual opportunities. Replay 72 prior max-age-400 screening assays on seed 84001 to establish source and run identity. All outcomes, including any unstable or weak runs, remain in the dataset. A sub-20 population is an operational bottleneck, not a test of genetic stability. If validation fails, report the failure and revise before the main campaign.

If validation passes, run 200 blocks of 72 assays each. First, reuse the previous 100 source seeds 85001–85100 and match their source panel and initial physical fingerprints to the earlier max-age-300 experiment. This gives paired seed-level lifespan comparisons. Second, use 100 new source seeds 87001–87100 to test independent replication. The five validation seeds and earlier candidate-screen seeds are excluded from the main results. Both sets use the same fixed source initialization and previous assay settings except the lifespan. Within each source and food/age setting verify equal initial states across thresholds after normalizing only the genomic threshold trait. Source panels, physical matching and raw states must be checked rather than assumed.

## Estimands

For each seed, founder-age mode, threshold and food define D as the mixture's population minus the mean of eight monocultures. Define F(q) = D(limited food) - D(original food) at reproductive threshold q, and T = F(80) - F(120). Primary results are T at tick 1,000 for fixed and staggered founders, reported separately for the paired 100-seed set and the independent 100-seed set. For the paired set also estimate the seed-wise change in T between lifespans 400 and 300. Report 95% paired-seed t intervals, the planned and actual denominators, and unadjusted multiplicity. Tick 200, 400, 600 and 800 results are secondary. A confidence interval spanning zero does not establish equivalence.

Track total births to parents younger than 50, 50–99, 100–149 and 150 or older, and calculate the corresponding food-by-threshold interactions in the mixture-minus-control birth counts as descriptive accounting contributions. Report young and older bands consistently with the prior study and compute paired lifespan differences using identical source seeds. These age bands are not a causal mediation analysis and may mask changes within the 150-or-older range, which now extends to age 399. Follow each individual's first eligible opportunity before age 50 to first birth, death or censoring and estimate 50-tick cumulative incidence by Aalen-Johansen, with a full-follow-up sensitivity; cohorts are post-treatment selected. Source seeds, not individual agents, are independent units. Record failed or absent cohorts without excluding their population outcomes.

Independently check pilot event-to-cohort reconstruction, age-band birth and death accounting, tick-level population ledgers, no-op replay, start fingerprints, source hashes, competing-risk estimates against `survival::survfit`, and raw-file checksums. Report per-group bottlenecks and cap exposure, including the high-threshold limited-food subgroup. Avoid interpreting a replicated direction or a non-significant interaction as proof of equivalence, or a descriptive age-band shift as a causal mechanism.

## Prespecified age-band replay added during execution

Before inspecting the main outcomes, the independent validation showed that 30–44% of births in the 150-or-older band were to parents 300 or older, depending on threshold and food. The old max-age-300 setting could not produce those births. To compare the same age range, replay all 200 main source blocks at lifespan 400 with the identical driver and gate, enabling the already validated read-only individual opportunity logging. Verify all run-level physical and panel hashes, population endpoints and age-birth totals exactly against their main-run counterparts. Independently split every 150-or-older birth into ages 150–299 and ages 300 or older from the recorded parent ages, then calculate the same seed-level interaction separately for those two bands and for parents under 50. Compare the 150–299 interaction against the earlier lifespan-300 150-or-older interaction within the 100 matched blocks. Retain the independent new-seed results separately. These additional contrasts are exploratory and unadjusted; they do not establish mediation. Enabling the observer is a replay of the same trajectories, not an additional independent replicate.

Estimate this 14,400-assay replay at 10–20 minutes based on the completed pilot and main campaign, including logging overhead; enforce a 20-minute deadline and register progress. Do not launch it until the main campaign has completed, preserving the 24-worker resource ceiling.
