# Young-parent reproduction dose-response pilot

Question: does restricting reproductive opportunities of young parents produce a graded change in realized birth timing and energy, and can the same individuals reproduce after a blocked opportunity? This is an exploratory pilot, not a mediation estimate or a powered test of the population interaction.

Use five fresh source seeds 81001–81005, previously unused by the age-reproduction experiment. Generate each source once with the previous haploid, 100-founder, threshold-80, mutation-0.2 source settings. Use eight source policies as before, 48 founders with age 50, two food rates (0.05 and 0.025, with corresponding initial food 0.5 and 0.25), and reproductive thresholds 80 and 120. Cross mixture and all eight balanced monocultures with young-age gate doses 0, 0.25, 0.5, 0.75, 1.0: 180 assays per source, 900 total, 1,000 ticks. Record failed sources and all assay outcomes; do not replace or discard them. Worlds have identical initial physical state within each source and food/threshold cell. Trajectories diverge after interventions.

Use the earlier gate at age <50, after energy, age and cap checks, before local space, mating and reproductive cost. A deterministic hash keyed by source, individual id and tick determines a nested fraction of opportunities blocked without consuming simulation RNG. Individual records cover every gate-eligible opportunity, including parents of other ages: tick, id, age, energy immediately before the gate, gate decision, births to that parent in that tick, and parent's energy at end of tick. Also save individual death ticks and endpoint survival. The event record describes opportunities, not guaranteed births. End-of-tick energy may include other same-tick interactions, so its change is not necessarily reproduction cost alone. For blocked events, follow the same individual to a later birth, death or end of run; distinguish censoring and multiple blocked opportunities. Associations within treated trajectories do not prove a counterfactual birth was postponed.

Summarize blocked fractions, realized young-parent births, birth ages, per-individual later births within 50 ticks among blocked opportunities with sufficient observation, and energy immediately before and after opportunities. Compare source-level means across doses, including a mixture-versus-eight-monoculture population contrast; five seeds imply high uncertainty. Sham no-op against the previous 71001 dataset must reproduce prior final population, births and deaths across matching cells. Check 900 runs, source and initial-state hashes, nested dose decisions, all births attributed to opportunity parents, per-tick and per-run conservation, endpoint viability, and survival/censoring accounting. Record exploratory interpretation and bottlenecks, with no selection on outcomes. All interventions are research-only patches to the pinned prior source.

Compute: Totoro, five workers, four Julia threads per worker, BLAS one; estimated 5–10 minutes from the previous 576-run, 106-second pilot plus per-individual I/O overhead. Stop if 10 minutes is exceeded and re-estimate. A short smoke test precedes the pilot.
